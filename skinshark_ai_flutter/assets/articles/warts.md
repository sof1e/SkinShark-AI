# Common Warts
![Warts](assets/images/warts.jpg)
Common warts are small, rough skin growths that usually appear on the fingers or hands. They often have a grainy texture and may show tiny black dots, which are clotted blood vessels.

These warts are caused by a virus (human papillomavirus, or HPV) and are spread by direct contact. It can take 2 to 6 months for a wart to develop after exposure. While common warts are generally harmless and may disappear on their own, some people choose to remove them if they are bothersome or embarrassing.

---

## Symptoms

- Small, fleshy, grainy bumps on the fingers or hands
- Rough texture
- Black dots (clotted blood vessels) on the surface

Consult a healthcare professional if:

- The warts hurt, bleed, burn, or itch
- Warts persist, spread, or return after treatment
- The growths are bothersome or interfere with daily activities
- You are unsure if the growths are warts
- You have many warts
- You have a weakened immune system
- Warts appear on the face, feet, or genitals

---

## Causes

Common warts are caused by certain types of HPV. The virus spreads through skin contact or by sharing objects like towels or nail clippers. It usually enters the skin through small cuts or breaks. Nail biting can also spread warts around the fingers.

Not everyone exposed to HPV will develop warts, as immune responses vary.

---

## Risk Factors

You may be at higher risk if you:

- Are a child or young adult
- Have a weakened immune system (e.g., HIV/AIDS, organ transplant)
- Bite your nails or pick at hangnails

---

## Prevention

To help prevent common warts:

- Avoid touching or picking at warts (yours or others’)
- Use separate nail care tools for warts and healthy skin
- Don’t bite your nails or pick at hangnails
- Groom carefully and avoid shaving areas with warts
- Avoid sharing towels, washcloths, or using public hot tubs and showers
- Use hand moisturizer to prevent dry, cracked skin

---

## Treatment

Most common warts go away on their own, but treatment can help speed up removal or relieve discomfort. Options include:

- **Over-the-counter remedies:** Salicylic acid (liquid, gel, or patch) applied daily
- **Cryotherapy:** Freezing the wart with liquid nitrogen at a clinic
- **Prescription treatments:** Stronger peeling medicines or topical immunotherapy
- **Minor procedures:** Laser therapy, minor surgery, or other removal techniques

Always consult a healthcare professional before starting treatment, especially for children, people with diabetes, or those with weakened immune systems.

## When to See a Doctor

Consult a healthcare provider if you notice persistent skin changes, discomfort, or if symptoms worsen.
