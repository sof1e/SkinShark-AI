## Skin cancer symptoms
![ABCDE](assets/images/moles.png)

## Overview

Skin cancer often develops in visible areas, making early detection possible. Regular self-exams for new or changing skin growths are important. If you notice anything unusual, consult a primary care physician, dermatologist, or qualified health care professional.

---

## What does skin cancer look like?

Skin cancer and precancerous conditions can appear in many forms. Watch for new or changing moles and use the **ABCDE** system to help identify suspicious spots:

- **A – Asymmetry:** One half of the mole looks different from the other.
- **B – Border:** Edges are uneven, notched, blurred, or ragged.
- **C – Color:** Uneven color, with shades of brown, black, tan, white, gray, pink, or blue.
- **D – Diameter:** Larger than a pea or changing in size.
- **E – Evolving:** Changes in size, shape, or color over time.

---

## Common symptoms

- Skin growths, scabs, or sores that don’t heal
- Changes in existing moles or spots
- Pale or waxy patches
- Scaly patches
- Red or flesh-colored raised moles

### Details on symptoms

- **Non-healing growths or sores:** May appear as nodules, rashes, or irregular patches. These can be raised, bleed, or ooze.
- **Changes in existing spots:** Monitor for changes in shape, color, or size, even if previously checked by a doctor.
- **Pale or waxy patches:** Basal cell carcinoma may look like a pale patch or waxy bump, sometimes with visible blood vessels or a central indentation.
- **Scaly patches:** Squamous cell carcinoma may form rough, firm lumps or reddish, scaly patches that persist.
- **Red or flesh-colored moles:** Merkel cell carcinoma often appears as rapidly growing, raised red or flesh-colored moles, usually on sun-exposed skin.

---

## Self-check tips

Regularly examine your skin for new or changing spots. Check all areas, including those not usually exposed to the sun: between toes, under nails, palms, soles, genitals, and even the eyes. Report anything suspicious to your health care provider.


## When to See a Doctor

Consult a healthcare provider if you notice persistent skin changes, discomfort, or if symptoms worsen.
