## What is Herpes?

**Herpes simplex virus (HSV)**, commonly called herpes, is a widespread infection that can cause painful blisters or ulcers. It spreads mainly through skin-to-skin contact. While treatable, it is not curable.

There are **two types** of herpes simplex virus:

- **Type 1 (HSV-1):**  
    - Mostly spreads by oral contact  
    - Causes infections in or around the mouth (oral herpes or cold sores)  
    - Can also cause genital herpes  
    - Most adults are infected with HSV-1

- **Type 2 (HSV-2):**  
    - Spreads by sexual contact  
    - Causes genital herpes

Most people have no symptoms or only mild symptoms. The infection can cause painful blisters or ulcers that may recur. Medicines can reduce symptoms but cannot cure the infection.

Recurrent symptoms of both oral and genital herpes may be distressing. Genital herpes may also be stigmatizing and impact sexual relationships.

---

## Symptoms

Most people with herpes have no symptoms or only mild symptoms. Many are unaware they have the infection and can unknowingly pass the virus to others.

**Symptoms may include:**

- Painful, recurring blisters or ulcers
- Fever, body aches, and swollen lymph nodes (especially with new infections)

Symptoms can differ between the first episode (or ‘outbreak’) and recurrent episodes. If symptoms occur, they often begin with tingling, itching, or burning near the area where sores will appear.

**Oral herpes symptoms:**

- Blisters (cold sores) or open sores (ulcers) in or around the mouth or lips

**Genital herpes symptoms:**

- Bumps, blisters, or open sores (ulcers) around the genitals or anus

These sores and blisters are typically painful. Blisters may break open, ooze, and then crust over.

**During the first infection, people may experience:**

- Fever
- Body aches
- Sore throat (oral herpes)
- Headache
- Swollen lymph nodes near the infection

People can have repeated outbreaks over time (‘recurrences’), which are usually shorter and less severe than the first outbreak.

---

## Causes

Herpes spreads through direct contact with an infected person’s skin or bodily fluids, especially during an outbreak. Transmission can occur through kissing, sexual contact, or sharing personal items.

---

## Diagnosis

A healthcare provider can diagnose herpes by examining the sores and may confirm with a swab test or blood test.

---

## Treatment

There is no cure for herpes, but antiviral medications can help:

- Manage symptoms
- Reduce the frequency of outbreaks
- Lower the risk of transmission

---

## Possible Complications

### HSV-2 and HIV Infection

- HSV-2 infection increases the risk of acquiring HIV infection by about three-fold.
- People with both HIV and HSV-2 are more likely to spread HIV.
- HSV-2 is among the most common infections in people living with HIV.

### Severe Disease

- In immunocompromised people (including those with advanced HIV), herpes can cause more severe symptoms and more frequent recurrences.
- Rare complications of HSV-2: meningoencephalitis (brain infection), disseminated infection.
- Rare complications of HSV-1: encephalitis (brain infection), keratitis (eye infection).

### Neonatal Herpes

- Can occur when an infant is exposed to HSV during delivery.
- Rare (about 10 out of every 100,000 births globally), but serious.
- Can lead to lasting neurologic disability or death.
- Risk is greatest when a mother acquires HSV for the first time in late pregnancy.

---

## Living with Herpes

- Avoid skin-to-skin contact during outbreaks
- Use condoms to reduce transmission risk
- Inform partners about your condition
- Manage stress and maintain a healthy lifestyle to reduce outbreaks

---

## When to See a Doctor

Consult a healthcare provider if you notice persistent skin changes, discomfort, or if symptoms worsen.
